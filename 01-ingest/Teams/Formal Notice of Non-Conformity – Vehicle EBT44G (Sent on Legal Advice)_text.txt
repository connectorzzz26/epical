Dear Daniel,







I am writing to you on the advice of my lawyer regarding
 the vehicle I purchased from you on 03/12/2025, registration number EBT44G.







After taking delivery of the vehicle, I discovered that the
 auxiliary heater installed in the car relies exclusively on 2G/3G mobile networks and was installed by a third party. This information was never disclosed to us prior to the sale.







Lack of conformity


As you are aware, 2G and 3G networks are being phased out
 and shut down across Europe. A heater that depends entirely on this obsolete infrastructure therefore has a built-in failure and is not fit for its intended purpose.







This constitutes a lack of conformity under EU consumer law,
 including Directive (EU) 2019/771 on goods with digital elements.







Serious cybersecurity and safety risk


In addition, I am a cybersecurity expert and architect, and
 I know for a fact that 2G/3G networks are inherently insecure and vulnerable to cyberattacks. These technologies lack modern security protections and are widely documented as exploitable.


By selling a vehicle equipped with a system that:



		
Depends on obsolete mobile networks, and

		
Exposes the vehicle to known cybersecurity vulnerabilities,




you placed us at risk of serious operational, security, and
 potential safety issues. This risk could have caused, or may already have caused, significant problems.


These risks were not disclosed, despite being material information
 that would reasonably affect a buyer’s decision.







Misleading omission


The failure to disclose both:



		
The dependence on obsolete 2G/3G technology, and

		
The resulting functional and security risks,




constitutes a misleading omission of material information
 under Directive 2005/29/EC (Unfair Commercial Practices).


The fact that the heater was installed by a third party is
 irrelevant. You sold the vehicle with this equipment included and are therefore legally responsible for the condition, conformity, and safety of the vehicle as sold.







Required remedy


Following legal advice, I formally require immediate corrective
 action. Given the nature of the defect and the lack of disclosure, this issue cannot be treated as minor.


I therefore require one of the following remedies, at your
 full cost and without delay:



		
Full compensation covering the complete removal and replacement of the heater with a compliant, secure, and future-proof system, including all parts, labor, configuration, and related costs, or

		
A substantial price reduction reflecting the loss of functionality, the security risk, and the misleading nature of the sale, or

		
Termination of the sales contract with a full refund of the purchase price and associated costs.




In addition to the remedy for the non-conforming hardware,
 I reserve all rights to claim additional damages, including but not limited to the cost of technical security assessment and mitigation measures, as well as the loss of use of the vehicle’s remote heating functionality during the resolution period.







Please provide your response in writing within 14 days from
 receipt of this email.







Next steps


If this matter is not resolved within the stated timeframe,
 I will proceed, as advised by my lawyer, with:



		
Formal complaints to the relevant consumer protection authorities, and

		
Further legal action under EU and national law.




I expect this matter to be handled seriously and in full
 compliance with your legal obligations.
