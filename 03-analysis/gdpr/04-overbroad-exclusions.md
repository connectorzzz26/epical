# Overbroad Exclusions And Misuse Of Article 15(4)

## Finding

The internal DSAR dossier uses categorical exclusion language for email, Teams, attachments, business information, and logs. That is too broad. GDPR Article 15(4) allows protection of the rights and freedoms of others, but it does not permit a blanket refusal to assess and provide the data subject's personal data.

## Evidence: Categorical Exclusions In The Internal Dossier

Evidence file:

- `Sharepoint\Thaer_DSAR_Dossier_With_HR_Details.docx`

Extracted lines:

```text
20: Scope of Access
21: The right of access applies strictly to personal data relating to Thaer Saidi. It does not include a right to receive:
22: - entire email mailboxes or raw system exports;
23: - full email conversations or Teams threads;
24: - attachments or documents containing business, customer, or project-related information without personal data;
25: - commercially sensitive or confidential business information.
26: Third-Party Personal Data
27: Information containing personal data about other individuals cannot be disclosed unless it is possible to separate or redact their data without affecting the meaning.
31: Business Confidentiality
32: Emails, attachments, and internal messages often contain trade secrets or commercially sensitive information ... Under GDPR Article 15(4), such information must not be disclosed if doing so would adversely affect the rights of the organisation or others.
33: System Logs and Security Data
34: Only log entries that constitute personal data relating to the data subject will be disclosed. Security logs containing internal security mechanisms or privileged operational data must be summarised or withheld where necessary to protect security.
```

## Why This Is A Strong GDPR Point

This language is defensible only if applied case-by-case. It becomes non-compliant if used as a blanket rule to avoid:

- emails where you are the subject;
- Teams messages where you are discussed;
- HR/legal documents about the investigation;
- audit/access logs tied to your user identity;
- attachments that contain your personal data;
- redacted copies or meaningful extracts.

The dossier does not show a granular assessment, redaction log, withholding schedule, or reasons tied to specific documents.

## Complaint Wording

Epical/Nordcloud relied on overbroad Article 15(4) exclusion language. Instead of identifying, redacting, and disclosing the data subject's personal data from emails, Teams, attachments, and logs, the internal dossier treats whole categories as excluded or summarised. This is inconsistent with the right of access unless the controller can demonstrate a document-by-document assessment and specific adverse effect.
