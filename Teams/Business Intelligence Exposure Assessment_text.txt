Executive Summary
The technical vulnerabilities identified in NCC's IFS Cloud and Azure Cosmos DB integration do not merely risk data loss—they expose high-value competitive intelligence to a small number of sophisticated actors who could extract hundreds of millions of SEK in competitive advantage annually. This assessment quantifies what threat actors actually gain and identifies the most likely beneficiaries.
	CRITICAL FINDING
The current architecture provides external consultants with sufficient access to extract project-level financial data, cost structures, subcontractor pricing, and operational metrics that would give Nordic competitors a decisive 0.5-3% bidding advantage—worth 50-400 MSEK per major tender. The value is concentrated among 5-25 key decision-makers at competitor firms, hyperscalers, and investment funds.

2. Threat Actor Analysis – Who Benefits and How Much
Tier 1: Nordic Construction Competitors
Primary Beneficiaries: Skanska, Peab, Veidekke, YIT, AF Gruppen
Attack Vector: Tender Intelligence
NCC competes in tenders worth hundreds of millions to billions of SEK for highways, rail extensions, hospitals, universities, metro projects, and wastewater treatment plants. In markets where the winner typically prevails by 0.5-3%, access to NCC's exact cost structures provides a decisive advantage.
What They Gain:
• Ability to price just below NCC while protecting margin
• Intelligence to walk away from tenders NCC will win anyway
• Knowledge of NCC's current hunger for work vs. margin discipline
• Understanding of NCC's risk pricing on difficult site conditions
Financial Impact:
50-400 MSEK per year per competitor (winning or avoiding 1-3 major tenders)
Attack Vector: Subcontractor Negotiation
Armed with NCC's exact supplier pricing, competitors can approach the same subcontractors and demand equivalent or better terms.
What They Gain:
• Leverage to negotiate: "We know you give NCC X SEK/m³ — match it or we walk"
• Knowledge of which suppliers offer extraordinary discounts or payment terms
• Direct gross margin improvement on every overlapping project
Financial Impact:
20-150 MSEK per year per competitor (margin improvement across portfolio)


Tier 2: Global Hyperscalers & Data Center Contractors
Primary Beneficiaries: Microsoft, Google, AWS, Oracle (hyperscalers); Large GCs pursuing data center work
Attack Vector: Nordic Data Center Execution Intelligence
NCC's real execution experience with the Meta/Facebook Luleå campus and other Nordic hyperscaler facilities provides invaluable benchmarks that are otherwise unavailable.
What They Gain:
• Real PUE (Power Usage Effectiveness) achieved in cold-climate builds
• Actual construction cost per MW (extremely valuable benchmark)
• Timeline slippage patterns: permitting, winter delays, grid connection
• Change-order frequency and magnitude from hyperscaler clients
• Sustainability premium tolerance (how much extra hyperscalers will pay for green features)
• Modular vs. traditional build trade-offs in Nordic conditions
Strategic Value:
Even if not directly competing with NCC for the next contract, this intelligence enables hyperscalers to negotiate harder with eventual winners or adjust internal budgeting for Nordic expansion. For general contractors, it provides a realistic cost and risk model to win more hyperscaler work.
Financial Impact:
50-300 MSEK strategic value (improved negotiation position and execution planning)


Tier 3: Investment Funds & Activist Investors
Primary Beneficiaries: Hedge funds, private equity, activist investors, banks/bondholders
Attack Vector: Financial Health Early Warning
Project-level financial data reveals which projects are underwater, delayed cash collection patterns, and provisions/write-downs coming in the next 1-2 quarters—information that precedes public financial statements by months.
What They Gain:
• Early positioning for short-selling opportunities
• Clearer M&A or spin-off valuation intelligence
• Ability to adjust credit exposure before earnings releases
• Backlog quality assessment (how much is low-margin or high-risk)
Financial Impact:
10-200 MSEK per year (trading profits and positioning)

The value is enormous, but only if the intelligence remains within a small circle. This is why sophisticated actors keep such access extremely restricted.




4. Technical Vulnerability → Business Intelligence Linkage
The technical vulnerabilities identified in the IFS Cloud and Cosmos DB integration directly enable business intelligence extraction:
	Technical Vulnerability
	Business Intelligence Exposure

	Over-privileged Consultant Access
	Consultants with delete/read permissions can extract complete project financials, subcontractor pricing, and operational metrics before triggering a rollback to cover tracks

	Broad Wipe-Out Logic
	Malicious actor can intentionally trigger date-level wipes to create gaps in audit trails, making data exfiltration harder to detect and trace

	Retry Storm Amplification
	Creates operational noise and incident response fatigue, providing cover for targeted data extraction during periods of system instability

	Lack of Granular Audit Controls
	Insufficient monitoring of consultant read patterns means systematic data extraction can occur over weeks or months without detection

6. Regulatory & Standards Compliance Impact
ISO/IEC 27001 – Information Security
The current architecture violates multiple ISO 27001 controls that are specifically designed to prevent competitive intelligence exposure:
• A.5.15 (Access Control): Over-privileged consultants can access sensitive competitive data
• A.8.10 (Information Deletion): Broad wipe-out logic enables audit trail destruction
• A.8.12 (Data Integrity): Phantom data loss risk compromises financial record reliability
• A.5.36 (Independent Review): No third-party audit of high-risk integration architecture
NIST Cybersecurity Framework
NIST CSF gaps directly enable business intelligence extraction:
• Identify (ID.RA): Integration logic risk not formally classified as competitive intelligence threat
• Protect (PR.AC / PR.DS): No protection against data exfiltration by privileged consultants
• Detect (DE.CM): No monitoring for systematic read patterns indicating intelligence gathering
• Recover (RC.RP): Cannot recover from targeted extraction once sensitive data is compromised


7. Immediate Actions Required
1. Emergency Access Audit
Timeframe: 48-72 hours
Immediately audit all consultant access to IFS Cloud and Cosmos DB over the past 12 months. Identify unusual read patterns, bulk data exports, or access immediately preceding major tender submissions.
2. Implement Zero Trust for Consultants
Timeframe: 1-2 weeks
Revoke broad delete and read permissions. Implement just-in-time access with 4-hour expiration for specific data sets. Add real-time alerting for bulk reads, cross-project queries, and subcontractor pricing access.
3. Refine Rollback Logic
Timeframe: 2-4 weeks
Move from date-level wipe-outs to record-level or transaction-scoped rollbacks. Eliminate the ability to trigger broad data destruction that obscures audit trails.
4. External Third-Party Audit
Timeframe: Engage within 1 week, complete in 4-6 weeks
Contract independent firm (e.g., EY, KPMG) to conduct forensic review of Azure IAM, RBAC, Cosmos DB access logs, and integration architecture. Focus on identifying any historical data exfiltration patterns.
5. Client Notification Assessment
Timeframe: Upon completion of access audit
If audit reveals evidence of systematic data extraction or if broad wipe-outs have occurred, initiate incident response protocol and assess notification obligations to affected clients and regulatory bodies.
